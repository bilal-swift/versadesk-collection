Buying Guide

Things to Know Before You Buy

A standing desk converter (also called a desk riser) sits on top of the desk you already own and lets you switch between sitting and standing in seconds. Here's what to look for.

What are the different types of standing desk converters?

Converters differ in three ways: how they lift, how the work surface is laid out, and how they sit on your desk. Knowing these helps you match a converter to the way you actually work.

Adjustment Mechanism

  • Electric / MotorizedVersaDesk
    A quiet motor raises and lowers the platform at the push of a button, with almost no physical effort. Needs a nearby outlet.
  • Gas-Spring / Pneumatic
    A squeeze lever releases a gas cylinder that counterbalances the load. Smooth and quiet, no power required.
  • Manual Spring or Crank
    Side handles, ratchets or a rotating crank. Usually the lowest cost, but takes more muscle and time to adjust.

Surface & Layout

  • Two-TierVersaDesk
    Monitors sit on the top shelf while keyboard and mouse sit on a lower tray, keeping wrists at a healthier angle.
  • CornerVersaDesk
    A V-shaped or angled design that fits neatly into an L-shaped desk or cubicle corner.
  • CompactVersaDesk
    A smaller footprint for minimal setups and narrow desks.
  • Flat-Top / Single Surface
    One platform holds both screen and keyboard, like a regular desk.

Mounting & Motion

  • FreestandingVersaDesk
    Sits directly on your desk with a stable base. No drilling or clamps, and easy to move to another room.
  • Clamp-On
    Attaches to the desk edge or a grommet hole. Saves surface space but takes installation and depends on desk thickness.
  • Vertical vs. Arcing Lift
    Straight-up X-frame lifts save desk depth. Arcing lifts swing toward you as they rise, so you need more clear space.
The VersaDesk approach: every converter in this collection pairs an electric motorized lift with a freestanding, two-tier design. You get push-button adjustment and a dedicated keyboard tray, with no clamps and no drilling.

How much weight can a standing desk converter hold?

A typical converter is rated for about 30–45 lbs, but capacity varies a lot by size and design. The rating tells you what the motor or spring can safely lift. Overload it and the converter becomes hard, or impossible, to raise.

How much height adjustment do you need?

Most converters adjust from about 4.5" to 20" above your desk. The top of that range decides whether you can stand comfortably. The bottom decides whether you can still sit well.

6.1" – 18.8"EvoLift™ height range (155–479 mm), with a 12 mm/s electric lift

Stepless vs. fixed levels

  • Stepless (infinite): stop at any point along the track for an exact fit. All VersaDesk electric converters adjust with no preset stopping points.
  • Fixed levels: some spring-assisted designs lock into a set number of slots, so your ideal height may fall between two settings.
  • Memory presets: PowerPro™ Elite models save your sit and stand heights, so you can switch with one touch.

Fit tips

  • Base height adds up. Fully lowered, a converter still adds a few inches to your desk. Factor this into your seated chair height.
  • Measure your elbows. Standing, your keyboard should sit at or just below elbow height.
  • Leave cable slack. Give power and data cords 10–12" of extra length so nothing pulls when you rise.

Ergonomic setup checklist

Good ergonomics means keeping your body in neutral postures, easing into standing, and setting the converter so both your screens and your hands are at the right height.

  1. Set your elbow angle

    Adjust the keyboard tray so your elbows bend at 90–100°, forearms parallel to the floor and shoulders relaxed.

  2. Position your monitor

    Put the top third of the screen at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away.

  3. Keep keyboard & mouse together

    Place both on the same tray, close together, to avoid overreaching and wrist strain.

  4. Stand tall, knees soft

    Spread your weight evenly across both feet and keep your knees slightly unlocked.

  5. Dial in screen height separately

    If your screen and keyboard can't both reach the right height, add a monitor arm through the VersaDesk grommet holes.

  6. Start slowly

    Begin with 15–30 minutes of standing per hour and build up as your body adjusts.

  7. Keep moving

    Shift your weight, make small movements and take short walking breaks instead of standing still for hours.

  8. Add an anti-fatigue mat

    A cushioned floor mat makes standing more comfortable, especially during the first few weeks.

Why choose a standing desk converter?

A converter is the fastest, most affordable way to get a sit-stand workstation without giving up the desk you already like.

Cost & Convenience

  • Saves money: costs far less than replacing your desk with a full electric standing desk.
  • Keeps your desk: no need to throw out furniture you already own and like.
  • Fast setup: minimal assembly, so you're working within minutes.
  • Portable: freestanding design moves easily to a new room or office.

Health & Comfort

  • Eases discomfort: alternating positions can reduce strain on your lower back, neck and shoulders.
  • Better circulation: standing gets blood flowing and helps prevent stiffness from long hours in a chair.
  • More movement: standing uses slightly more energy than sitting still.

Focus & Energy

  • Beat the afternoon slump: changing positions helps fight fatigue without an extra coffee.
  • Stay alert: regular movement helps you stay focused and engaged while you work.
  • Build a habit: PowerPro™ Elite app reminders prompt you to switch between sitting and standing.

FAQ

Standing Desk Converter Questions, Answered

What is a standing desk converter?
A standing desk converter, also called a desk riser, is a height-adjustable platform that sits on top of your existing desk. It lets you switch between sitting and standing without replacing your desk. VersaDesk converters use an electric motor, so you change position at the push of a button.
Is an electric converter better than a gas-spring or manual one?
Gas-spring and manual models need no power, but you lift with your own strength using levers, handles or a crank. Electric converters raise and lower the full load smoothly with a button, with no physical effort, and adjust to any height in their range. Many electric models also add extras like USB charging and memory presets. The only requirement is a nearby outlet.
How much weight can a VersaDesk converter hold?
Every PowerPro™ and PowerPro™ Elite converter, including the corner models, is rated for up to 80 lbs, enough for multi-monitor setups with arms and accessories. The compact EvoLift™ supports up to 40 lbs, ideal for a laptop and one or two monitors. Keep heavier items near the center of the platform for the smoothest lift.
How high does it go, and will it work for my height?
PowerPro™ models rise up to 20" above your desk, which comfortably suits standing users up to about 6'2" on a standard-height desk. The EvoLift™ adjusts from 6.1" to 18.8" (155–479 mm). All VersaDesk electric converters are stepless, so you can stop at any height in between.
Will a converter take up a lot of desk space?
VersaDesk converters lift straight up instead of swinging outward, so they don't need extra depth in front of your desk. Choose a width close to your monitor setup: 32" for compact spaces, 36–40" for one or two monitors, and 48" for wide multi-monitor layouts. For L-shaped desks or cubicles, the 36" x 31" corner models fit neatly into the corner.
Can I use dual or triple monitors?
Yes. PowerPro™ and PowerPro™ Elite converters have built-in grommet holes that accept standard monitor mounts, so you can run dual or triple screens. A monitor arm also lets you set screen height separately from the keyboard tray for better ergonomics.
How long should I stand each day?
Start with 15–30 minutes of standing per hour and build up gradually. The goal is to alternate, not to stand all day. Shift your weight, take short walking breaks and use an anti-fatigue mat for comfort. PowerPro™ Elite owners can set sit-stand reminders in the VersaDesk mobile app.
Does a converter change my seated position?
Fully lowered, a converter still adds a few inches to your desk height. When you sit, raise your chair slightly so your elbows stay at 90–100° and your feet rest flat on the floor or on a footrest.
Is assembly required?
Very little. VersaDesk converters arrive nearly ready to use. Set the unit on your desk, attach the keyboard tray if needed, plug it in, and you can be working within minutes.
